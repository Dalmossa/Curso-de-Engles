## FASE 1 — Input (i+1) [4 min]

### Vocabulary (with PT-BR gloss)

| English | PT-BR | Register note |
|---------|-------|---------------|
| **CEO** — Chief Executive Officer | Diretor-executivo (o topo) | say the letters, then the expansion |
| **CTO** — Chief Technology Officer | Diretor de tecnologia | owns the technical direction |
| **VP** — Vice President | Vice-presidente | one level under C-level |
| **Head of Engineering** | Head de Engenharia | `Head of` + area; common in EU firms |
| **Tech Lead** | Lead técnico | the person you pair with; **not** a manager |
| **Engineering Manager** | Gerente de Engenharia | manages *people*, not the code |
| **line manager** | gerente direto | the one who signs your review |
| **report to** | responder a (hierarquia) | *Who do you report to?* |
| **DRI** — Directly Responsible Individual | responsável direto pela tarefa | used in stand-ups |

### Mini-text (authored)

> **Ana (new backend dev):** "So — who is my **DRI** for the payments migration?"
>
> **Kenji (tech lead):** "Technically, I am. I am your **tech lead**, so I unblock you. But I don't
> approve your time off."
>
> **Ana:** "So who does?"
>
> **Kenji:** "You **report to** Priya, the **Engineering Manager** for the payments squad. She
> **reports to** the **VP** of Platform. Above the VP sits the **CTO** — she is the only **C-level**
> I report to."
>
> **Ana:** "So the **CEO** is above her?"
>
> **Kenji:** "Yes, but you will never need to address the **CEO**. That is the whole point of the
> ladder: it tells you how formal to be. You say *Kenji*, *Priya*, *the VP*. You say *the CTO*."

> **What the dialogue is actually teaching.** A title is not decoration — it predicts register. The
> same person is *Kenji*, *your tech lead*, or *the CTO* depending on which rung you are standing
> on. This is why an email to "the CTO" reads as a formal escalation and an email to "Kenji" does
> not, even if the body is identical.

## FASE 2 — Guided Practice [5 min]

### Item 1 — Vocabulary (MCQ)
**CTO** is the correct expansion of which letters?
| Slot | Value |
|------|-------|
| A | Chief Technical Officer |
| B | **Chief Technology Officer** |
| C | Chief Testing Officer |
| D | Company Technical Operations |
**Key:** B
**br_pt_form:** "Diretor de Tecnologia"

### Item 2 — Vocabulary (MCQ)
Ana asks *"So who does?"* — approve my time off. Kenji answers: *"Priya, the **Engineering
Manager**."* What is the contrast Kenji is drawing?
| Slot | Value |
|------|-------|
| A | Priya is less senior than Kenji |
| B | Kenji has no authority over anyone |
| C | **Managing people and technical work are two different jobs** |
| D | Priya is a customer |
**Key:** C
**br_pt_form:** "gerenciar pessoas e trabalho técnico são trabalhos diferentes"

### Item 3 — Vocabulary (fill-in)
Complete: "I am your **tech lead**" = I unblock you. "I am not your **line manager**" = ______ .
| Slot | Value |
|------|-------|
| A | I do not sign your performance review |
| B | **I do not approve your time off or your salary** |
| C | I do not exist |
| D | I do not work with you |
**Key:** B
**br_pt_form:** "eu não aprovo suas férias nem seu salário"

### Item 4 — Register (MCQ) — the item that tests the lesson's actual point
Which is appropriate in a Slack message to the **VP** about a production incident?
| Slot | Value |
|------|-------|
| A | "Hey VP, heads up — prod is down lol, can you take a look when you get a sec?" |
| B | "**Hi [VP's first name] — the payments API is returning 500s since 09:14. I'm the DRI and I'm on it. I'll update you every 30 minutes.**" |
| C | "Dear sir, I would like to inform you that a system failure is ongoing." |
| D | "VP: the API. 500s. 09:14. mine. updates 30min." |
**Key:** B
**br_pt_form:** "Oi [nome] — a API de pagamentos está retornando 500 desde 09:14. Sou o responsável direto e estou cuidando. Atualizo a cada 30 minutos."

> **Why B and not A or C.** A is under-formal for someone two rungs up — the incident content is
> right, the register is wrong. C is over-formal: it treats an internal VP as external
> correspondence, and "Dear sir" presumes a gender the message never established. D is terse to
> the point of ambiguity: whose API, which status codes, and 30 minutes for what?

## FASE 3 — Output Task [4 min]

### Speaking: "Where does this person sit?" [60 seconds, recorded]

You have just joined a Brazilian company with an international engineering org. Your manager asks:
**"Before we continue — who is your tech lead, and who do you report to?"**

**Requirements (all mandatory):**
1. Name **one** role in each of two rungs: your immediate technical unblocker, and the person who
   approves leave.
2. Use **one** C-level or VP title to locate the top of your chain, correctly expanded.
3. Say **one** sentence on how that ladder changes how you address people.

**Suggested frame (not mandatory):** immediate role → line manager → one rung above → what that
means for how I write.

**Success rubric (CEFR A2→B1 Speaking + register):**
- Names the two roles with the correct *division of labour* (unblock vs. approve), not just the words
- Expands the acronym once, correctly
- Contains one register statement ("I use first names up to the VP, titles above that")
- No PT-speaker interference: `*report with`, `*approves me` — the correct forms are *I report to X*,
  *she approves my time off*

**Common L1 interference to pre-empt:** PT-PT speakers say *"eu reporto para ela"* or *"ele é meu
responsável"* for the manager. English requires the preposition *to* and possessive *my*: *I report
to her*, *she is my manager*, *I report to my manager*.

| D | Design Review Interface |
**Key:** B

### Item 2 — Grammar (L1, adaptive)
Choose the correct preposition: "I ______ Priya."
| Slot | Value |
|------|-------|
| A | report for |
| B | report with |
| C | **report to** |
| D | report at |
**Key:** C
**br_pt_form:** "eu reporto à Priya" — o português exige *a*, o inglês exige *to*
