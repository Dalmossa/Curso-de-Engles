## FASE 1 — Input (i+1) [4 min]

### The four categories (authored)

| Category | Question it answers | Parts | English verb |
|----------|--------------------|-------|--------------|
| **Input** | what goes *in* | keyboard, mouse, monitor, microphone, scanner | *plug in, connect* |
| **Output** | what comes *out* | monitor, speakers, printer, headphones | *plug in, print* |
| **Storage** | where it is *kept* | SSD, hard drive, USB stick, memory card | *save, copy, back up* |
| **Processing** | where it is *worked out* | CPU, GPU | *run, compute* |

### The word that breaks the categories: **peripheral**

A **peripheral** is anything attached to the computer that is **not** the computer itself. The trap
for a PT speaker is that *mouse* and *monitor* sit in **two** categories at once — a monitor is an
output device *and* a peripheral. The categories describe a **function**; *peripheral* describes a
**location relative to the box**. They are different questions, and English lets you ask both:

> "Is a monitor output?" — Yes, it is an **output device**.
> "Is a monitor a peripheral?" — Yes, it is a **peripheral**.

### Mini-text (authored)

> **Yara (support trainee):** "The customer says his **printer** stopped working. So the
> **peripheral** is broken?"
>
> **Tom (support):** "Maybe. But ask two questions first. Does the **monitor** work? If the monitor
> has no image either, it is not the printer — it is the **system unit**."
>
> **Yara:** "And if the monitor works but the printer does not?"
>
> **Tom:** "Then check the **cable**, the **port**, and the **driver**. A peripheral fails in one of
> three places: the cable, the port, or the software. Never open the printer on the first call."

> **What the dialogue teaches.** *Peripheral* is a triage word, not a label. It tells you the fault
> is outside the box, and therefore that the three things to check are physical before you ask
> anyone to reinstall anything.

## FASE 2 — Guided Practice [5 min]

### Item 1 — Vocabulary (MCQ)
Which of these is a **storage** device?
| Slot | Value |
|------|-------|
| A | The monitor |
| B | The microphone |
| C | **The SSD** |
| D | The speakers |
**Key:** C
**br_pt_form:** "SSD / unidade de estado sólido"

### Item 2 — Grammar: `there is` / `there are` (MCQ)
Choose the correct form: "______ two monitors and ______ one mouse on the desk."
| Slot | Value |
|------|-------|
| A | There is / there is |
| B | **There are / there is** |
| C | There have / there have |
| D | It is / it is |
**Key:** B
**br_pt_form:** "Há dois monitores e há um mouse na mesa"

> **Interference note.** Portuguese *há* is number-neutral, so PT speakers produce *"there have two
> monitors"* or *"there is two monitors"*. The agreement is not optional in English: the verb agrees
> with the **first noun after the number**, and the verb is singular after *there is*.

### Item 3 — Grammar: countability + irregular plural (MCQ)
"A desk has one mouse and two ______ ."
| Slot | Value |
|------|-------|
| A | mouses |
| B | **mice** |
| C | mices |
| D | mouse |
**Key:** B
**br_pt_form:** "um mouse e dois mice"

> **Interference note.** *mouse* is one of the few everyday countable nouns with a Germanic
> irregular plural (*mouse → mice*, *foot → feet*, *tooth → teeth*). In IT English it is the only
> one learners meet often. Do not generalise: *mouse* is irregular, *monitor* and *keyboard* are
> regular (*two keyboards*, never *two keymice*).

### Item 4 — Vocabulary (MCQ)
Tom says a peripheral fails in one of three places: *"the cable, the port, or the software."* What
is a **port** here?
| Slot | Value |
|------|-------|
| A | A number that identifies a network computer |
| B | **A socket on the computer that a cable plugs into** |
| C | The folder a file is saved into |
| D | A USB brand |
**Key:** B
**br_pt_form:** "porta"

## FASE 3 — Output Task [4 min]

### Speaking: "Triage this call" [90 seconds, recorded, pairs]

**Setup.** A colleague forwards you a support ticket. You must decide, out loud, in the order a
support engineer would: **is it a peripheral, and if so where is it failing?**

**Three tickets.** Choose one, or do all three if time allows.

**Ticket 1.** *"My mouse moves but nothing happens when I click. The keyboard types fine."*
**Ticket 2.** *"My monitor is black. The tower has power — the light is on."*
**Ticket 3.** *"My USB stick works on my laptop but not on the work desktop. Same port, same stick."*

**Requirements (all mandatory):**
1. Say whether the failing part is a **peripheral** or inside the **system unit**, and say why.
2. Name **two** of the three failure points — cable, port, driver/software — in the order you would
   check them.
3. Give **one** concrete next action ("I would ask the customer to…").

**Success rubric (CEFR A2→B1 Speaking + countability):**
- Correct peripheral/system-unit split with a reason, not just a label
- `there is` / `there are` agreement correct, including where the first noun is plural
- Plurals correct: *two mice* if Ticket 1, *two monitors* if Ticket 2, *one USB stick* if Ticket 3
- Exactly two failure points, in a defensible order, with *the* before singular nouns and no article
  before plurals

**What each ticket is really testing.** Ticket 1 is a **driver** problem disguised as a hardware
problem — the peripheral is fine, the software is not. Ticket 2 is the reverse: the peripheral is
fine, the system unit is not. A learner who answers both with "check the cable" has not
categorised anything.
