> **Provenance of the gender assignments.** The transcripts state gender for **7 of the 13 named
> speakers** (Ana, Ben, Luiza, Tom, Rafael, Bea, Nina, Omar, Sarah, Priya, Ahmed, Marco, Carla, Diego,
> Elena, Nadia — stated by name choice or by pronoun). For **6 speakers the text does not state it**
> — `Lead`, `Reviewer`, `Architect` in B1, and the two `Agent`/`User` labels in `dlg_a2_04_bug_report`.
> Those six were assigned by me to make the panel balance work, and the assignment is a **design
> choice about the recording, not a claim about the character**. If the dialogues are re-cast, the
> voice map must be regenerated by the §8.5 script, not edited by hand — which is precisely why the
> script exists.

#### The defect this exposed, stated accurately

The first draft of this index asserted a `v_a`–`v_d` panel and a separate `v_f`/`v_m` speaker pair,
and wrote a claim ("A1 has no panel voice") that was **false** — A1 always had all four panel voices.
That claim came from reading a *hand-written* matrix instead of generating it.

The real defect was the opposite, and worse. **Voice was not pinned to speaker.** `dlg_a1_03` and
`dlg_a1_04` assigned all four panel voices across a two-speaker dialogue, so *who was speaking was
not recoverable from the voice*. That silently breaks every attribution item, and it breaks Q-L5's
purpose rather than violating its letter. `verify-integrity.py` check 5g now enforces it
mechanically: a dialogue may not use more distinct voices than it has speakers.

> **Amendment 2026-09-30 (propagated to `listening-corpus-spec.md` §2.1): Control 1 is a BETWEEN-
> dialogue property.** Within a dialogue, voice is *pinned to the speaker* and is confounded with
> speaker identity by design — and that confound is the point, because it is what makes attribution
> tracking possible (`lst_lexical_007`: which of two phrases *this speaker* used, when both are in
> the audio). Voice balance is therefore achieved by **which voices a dialogue is assigned**, not by
> re-voicing items inside it. The rules restate as:
>
> | Was | Now |
> |-----|-----|
> | no voice on > 25% of a learner's items | **no single dialogue supplies > 30% of a learner's listening items** |
> | voice and target skill uncorrelated | each panel voice appears in **≥ 2 levels and ≥ 6 dialogues**, and spans **≥ 2 item types** |
>
> **Residual risk that this does not fix:** gender is perfectly confounded with voice
> (`v_a`/`v_c` always female), so a learner who is uncomfortable with a same-gender or
> different-gender voice cannot be separated from the voice effect at all. That is a deliberate
> simplification for a 4-voice panel, and it is recorded as a limitation rather than presented as
> balance. A real panel would need 8 voices with crossed gender. **DEF-14**, non-blocking.

### L2-2 — `lst_prn_h_001` · **[h] presence** · A1 · **GATED** · voice `v_c+v_d` (both clips)
**Status:** shipped (repaired) — §9 DEF-1 · rewritten

**Which word do you hear?** — from line 2, *"I have my things _____"*

| Slot | Value | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| A | air | [ɛə] — **no [h]** |
| B | **here** ✓ | [hɪə] — **[h] present** |
| C | ear | [ɪə] — **no [h]** |
| D | near | [nɪə] — **no [h]** |

**Gated on:** Q-L1, Q-L6, Q-L9. **Sub-bank renamed.** The item tests **presence of [h]** rather than a
minimal pair, because the two minimal-pair sets available for this target are both homophone sets —
*hair/hare* and *here/hear* — and a listener who hears the clip correctly cannot choose between them.

**Interference:** PT *aqui* has no [h] counterpart; Brazilian English inserts [h] into Spanish-derived
words (*arreglo*, *hotel*). The learner must hear whether the [h] is there at all.

**§9 DEF-1: original version withdrawn, this version ships.** The withdrawn version used
*hair / hare / here / her* — three of which are homophones of the key. It was flagged in place but
the revised option set was never written down, which would have left a broken item in the bank with a
promise attached. It is now written down. **This is the third time the same class of error occurred
(DEF-1, DEF-7 ×2, DEF-12); the consolidated rule is in §10.1.**

## 7. SOURCING AND REUSE BASIS OF THE CORPUS

*Added 2026-09-30. Two dialogue headers pointed at "§7 sourcing note" before this section existed —
a dangling reference that also happened to be the exact mislabelling the licensing section exists to
prevent. Both problems are fixed here.*

### 7.1 The rule for this corpus

| Aspect | Status |
|--------|--------|
| Transcript text | **`authored`.** All 2,005 words of the 13 dialogues were written for this repository. |
| Reproduction from the 7 local PDFs | **none.** No sentence, no phrase list, no respelling. |
| Reuse basis of the local PDFs | **`cite_only`** — they are *bibliography*: they decided **what** to teach and **where** a BR-PT learner will struggle. They are not the text basis. |
| Provenance label for the CAT | `authored` → admissible only inside an `authored_reviewed_corpus` that has passed the `listening-corpus-spec.md` §3 QA gate. |

The distinction matters mechanically: *"the register came from a chapter about meetings"* describes
a **decision about register**, and a register is not copyrightable. *"the sentence came from the
chapter"* describes reproduction, which is what the three BaixeLivros notices forbid. Every dialogue
header in this file must express the first and never the second.

### 7.2 The two headers that were wrong

| Dialogue | What it said | Problem |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| `dlg_a2_01_open_meeting` | *"Register drawn from `Dicionario...md` Ch4 (Reuniões) — see §7 sourcing note."* | Names a **restricted** source (`external-sources.md` §0.5 — explicit BaixeLivros no-redistribution notice) as the **text basis** of a transcript. "Drawn from" reads as derivation. |
| `dlg_a2_04_bug_report` | *"`Dicionario...md` Ch10 (Mensagens e chat) and Ch8; the *bug* sense from §11.1 item 33."* | Same, plus it implies a term-level derivation from a 40-item deck that is itself only `authored`-rebuilt in `content-map.md` §11.1 — so the provenance chain pointed at a restricted source *through* an authored intermediate. |

Both now read **"Register vocabulary selected with reference to …"** and carry an explicit
`cite_only` line. The term set is the same; the claim about it is now true.

### 7.4 The rule for the next dialogue

1. Write the transcript first. If a sentence is recognisable as coming from a source, it is the
   wrong sentence.
2. State the **scoping decision** and its source, using the wording "selected with reference to".
3. If the dialogue's content would be a **list from a source** — the failure mode that makes
   `cite_only` impossible, per `external-sources.md` §7.0 — it cannot be authored this way at all.
   That applies to the planned `dlg_a2_06_intro_three_way` only insofar as its *register* comes from
   a book; the schema it must make audible is a social convention, not a source's list.

### 8.4 The [θ] cluster is not four independent measurements

`lst_prn_th_001`, `_002`, `_010`, `_011` all test [θ] presence, at A1 and B1, in a numeral frame
(*three years*, *three months*, *three services*). Three of the four use **the same carrier frame and
the same distractor set** (tree / free / street).

**Consequence for the calibration record:** a learner who passes all four has passed one contrast
four times. The cluster is **intentional** — the CAT needs to locate the A1→B1 transition on the
highest-value BR-PT contrast, and a single item cannot locate a transition — but the cluster must be
reported as **one construct with four observations**, not as four independent skills. Stated here so
`diagnostic-spec.md` §3.3a (dimensionality, n ≥ 5 per θ) is not applied to it naively.

**A fifth [θ] item exists and is deliberately *not* in this cluster.** `lst_prn_th_012`
(`dlg_a2_06_intro_three_way`) tests the same phone in an /əʊθ/ environment — *both* / *boat* — with a
distractor set (*boat / bath / moth*) that shares no member with *tree / free / street*. Two reasons
it stays outside: a cluster earns its name from a **shared carrier and a shared distractor set**, and
a second item in a different frame is a genuinely different observation rather than a fifth repetition
of the first four. It still does not count as an independent measurement of the [θ] construct, so the
construct now has **five observations in two clusters of different sizes** — four and one — and the
figure that matters for `diagnostic-spec.md` §3.3a is that they are **not five measurements**, not
that there are five of them.

### 8.5 Reproducing the measurement

```bash
python3 - <<'PY'
import re, pathlib
from collections import Counter
t = pathlib.Path('listening-corpus.md').read_text()
blocks = re.split(r'\n## `(dlg_[a-z0-9_]+)`\n', t)
idx = re.search(r'\| Dialogue \| Level \|.*?\|\n\|[-| ]+\|\n(.*?)\n\n', t, re.S).group(1)
declared = set(re.findall(r'`(dlg_[a-z0-9_]+)`', idx))
present  = {blocks[i] for i in range(1, len(blocks), 2)}
print("declared but absent:", declared - present)   # must be empty
ids = re.findall(r'### L\d-\d+ — `(lst_\w+?_\d+)`', t)
print("items:", len(ids), "unique:", len(set(ids)),
      "dupes:", [i for i in set(ids) if ids.count(i) > 1])
for i in range(1, len(blocks), 2):
    m = re.search(r'\*\*Transcript[^*]*:\*\*\n\n(.*?)\n\n---', blocks[i+1], re.S)
    lines = [l for l in m.group(1).split('\n') if l.strip().startswith('>')]
    w = len(re.findall(r"[A-Za-z'’\-]+",
          ' '.join(re.sub(r'^>\s*\*\*[A-Za-z ]+:\*\*\s*', '', l.lstrip('> '))
                   for l in lines)))
    print(f"{blocks[i]:<28} {w:>5}w {round(w/150*60):>4}s"
          f"  {'OK' if 45 <= round(w/150*60) <= 120 else 'OUT'}")
PY
```

The first line is the check that was missing when the index claimed 12 dialogues and 10 existed.
**Run it on every corpus change; a generated inventory is the only inventory that cannot lie.**

### 9.1 The classes, and why the counts matter

| Class | Instances | Shared root cause |
|-------|-----------|------------------|
| **Option not a real English word** | DEF-6 ×3 | A fourth option was invented to fill the slot, not found in a vocabulary list |
| **Two or more valid keys** | DEF-3, DEF-5, DEF-8, DEF-9, DEF-11 | The item was written from a *distractor* backwards, without checking the transcript |
| **Contrast absent in the audio channel** | DEF-1, DEF-7 ×2, DEF-12 | The contrast was chosen because it looks like a minimal pair in **spelling** |
| **Wrong sub-bank label** | DEF-10 | Sub-bank assigned by position, not by contrast type |
| **Voice not pinned to speaker** | DEF-13 | Panel balancing was applied *inside* dialogues, where it breaks speaker identity |

**Two of these classes are about the item's own construction and two are about the index.** DEF-13
is the most interesting because it was **invisible while the index was hand-written** and appeared
the moment the inventory was generated from the file. That is the same lesson as the missing B1
dialogues (§8.1): *a hand-maintained inventory of a generated artifact is a claim, not a record.*

**9 of the 12 item-level defects were caught by two mechanical checks that cost seconds each:**

1. Is every option a word in the corpus vocabulary list?
2. Does exactly one option occur in the carrier sentence or its two-turn window?
3. Does the intended contrast survive in the audio channel (i.e. is the key **not a homophone** of a distractor)?
4. Is the sub-bank determined by the *type* of contrast?

Neither the `tier-b-review.md` four-criteria pass (C1–C4) nor careful reading caught these, because
they are not linguistic errors. They are **table-construction errors**, and they are the same errors
that the `assessment-item-spec.md` §5.2 quality flags exist to catch at calibration. **The finding is
that a spec's validation gates are worth writing down only if something fails them** — the B1
dialogues missing entirely, and four dialogues failing Q-L2, is what makes §3's gate list credible
rather than decorative.

### 9.2 The corpus is not yet reviewable, and I will not pretend otherwise

Every defect above is a **text-level** defect, and text-level defects are the ones a careful author can
find. What this pass cannot find:

- whether a TTS render actually contains [θ] (Q-L1) — needs an ear
- whether a rendered clip is intelligible at 1.0× (Q-L3) — needs an ear
- whether an inference item is recoverable when heard rather than read (Q-L9 in audio)
- whether any of the `lst_inference` items are **too hard**, as opposed to merely subtle

So the correct status is the one stated at the top of this file: the transcripts are finished and
reviewed; the corpus is **not** `authored_reviewed_corpus`; and 19 L2 items are **authored and
pending**, not ready.
